My Frustration For You — Educate Yourself, Demand More

Blog

Migrated from the live site's blog archive.

Draft — Compliance Review Required

Discloses specific mutual-fund share-class fee percentages and a $1 million institutional-share minimum, plus fee-only/fiduciary positioning and a direct solicitation to contact the firm.

Since January of this year, we have been working hard to reduce allocations around long-duration bonds. Protecting our clients' money, increasing yield, and seeking some growth is not an easy task in this current investing environment. We pride ourselves on being fee-only and offering our clients a flat fee — there are three things that will eat away at a client's wealth: taxes, inflation, and fees, including advisory fees.

Because I am a fee-only investment advisor, I vow not to take commissions and trails from the investments I choose. I charge my clients a flat rate each year for my objective investment advice. I am their fiduciary.

Share classes, plainly

  • Class A Shares — front-end charge to you, sometimes as high as 3–5% off your initial investment; the advisor collects that as commission plus a small annual trail (typically about .25%).
  • Class B Shares — no front-end charge, but a back-end charge if you sell.
  • Class C Shares — a level charge each year, typically 1%, paid to the advisor.
  • Institutional Class Shares — a flat, simple charge each year around .65%, with no commission or trail collected by the advisor.

Guess what?

In order to invest in some institutional shares, you need a minimum of $1 million dollars. How does the prudent retail investor muster that? I got to thinking — we need a fee revolution. Consumers should demand answers: how do you get paid, in how many ways, how can I get fees waived, and how can minimums come down so I can participate in reasonably priced investments?

For independent financial and investment advice, feel free to contact us. We offer a "second look" on your portfolio on an hourly fee basis.

My best, Jennifer
