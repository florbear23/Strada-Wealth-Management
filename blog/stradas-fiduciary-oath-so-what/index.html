Strada's Fiduciary Oath — So What?

Blog

Migrated from the live site's blog archive.

Draft — Compliance Review Required

Direct fiduciary/fee-only positioning language — exactly the category this project already holds for outside counsel review everywhere else on the site.

Not a month goes by when I open an industry magazine and find articles asking the same three questions: What is a fiduciary? Who is a fiduciary? And who claims to be one? There is so much internal churn about how we, as investment professionals, present ourselves — brokers, advisors, financial planners, wealth managers — yet the public lacks understanding of these terms.

Who is an advisor?

Those registered with the SEC under the Investment Advisers Act should identify themselves as Advisors, potentially also using titles like Financial Planner, Investment Professional, or Wealth Manager.

Who is a broker?

A non-independent broker works for a wire house firm or multi-branch operation. Technically, anyone working for a brokerage firm should be calling themselves brokers; their primary loyalty is to their broker-dealer, not the client, and they are non-independent.

Who is a fiduciary?

A fiduciary is an individual in whom another has placed the utmost trust and confidence to manage and protect property or money — an obligation to act for another's benefit. Wirehouses created "fee-based" terminology to obscure the distinction from "fee-only," confusing investors about compensation structures.

Strada's fiduciary oath

Strada adopted a fiduciary oath based on Harold Evensky's original version. I recommend requesting that advisors sign a fiduciary oath on company letterhead to clarify compensation alignment. It is okay to pay for advice — we all have to make a living — just know how you are paying for it and where your professional advisor's interests align.
