Are My Dollar Bills Transparent?

In a financial advisory relationship, it is important for investors to understand the multitude of fees they may be required to pay.

When you walk into your CPA's or attorney's office, you sign an engagement letter, and pay a fee for their services; there are not a lot of hidden costs. With your financial advisor, that may not necessarily be the case.

If you ask your financial advisor, "What is your fee?" the common response is, "a percentage of your Assets Under Management (AUM)." So, if your advisor manages $1 million dollars, you pay $10,000 a year; some advisors charge more and some charge less. A typical fee was reported as 1.5% of AUM (Investment News).

A prudent investor seeking transparency will ask about other potential fees, such as: management fees; sub-manager fees; sales charges; transaction costs; custodian fees; performance fees; mortality and expense fees (in commission-based annuities); mutual fund expenses; operational expenses; and surrender fees.

My charge to you as an individual investor is to know what you are paying and why. My charge to the financial industry is that all fees become transparent. Over a 20- to 25-year investment period, fees can add up and cost investors a lot of money.

As an investor, you can't control what the markets are going to do, but you can control the information you have when you're doing your own investing: minimizing your taxes, keeping fees low and staying ahead of inflation.

Our firm prides itself on giving you all the information so you can make informed decisions that best suit your needs.
