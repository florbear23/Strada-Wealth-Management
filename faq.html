01 Financial & Retirement Planning

  • Financial planning involves creating a roadmap for your financial future, covering aspects like savings, investments, taxes, retirement, and estate planning, tailored to meet your long-term goals.

  • Retirement planning is crucial for ensuring you have the necessary resources to support yourself and maintain your desired lifestyle in retirement, without the reliability of a regular paycheck. A lot of people think retirement brings joy, but we see it often causes stress. The irregularity of not knowing where your income comes from is disconcerting. We work with you to manage that transition.

  • Ideally, you should review your financial plan annually or when significant life events occur, such as marriage, the birth of a child, a career change, or inheritance. We meet our clients as often as they want to meet and review their individual circumstances.

  • The sooner, the better. Starting in your 20s or as soon as you begin earning an income allows more time for your investments to grow through the power of compounding interest. But let’s be honest — many come to us later in their 40s, because life gets in the way. It is never too late.

  • A 401(k) is a retirement savings plan offered by employers that often includes a match to your contributions — even if you are your own employer. An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is a retirement savings account you open independently, offering tax advantages for your retirement savings.

  • Social Security provides a foundation of income in retirement but shouldn’t be relied upon as the sole source. It’s meant to complement your retirement savings and pension.

  • Inflation reduces the purchasing power of your money over time, meaning you’ll need more money in the future to maintain the same standard of living, making it crucial to plan for inflation in your retirement savings strategy.

  • Estate planning is preparing for the management and distribution of your assets after your death. It’s a critical component of a financial plan, ensuring your assets are distributed according to your wishes and your loved ones are provided for.

02 Divorce & Marital Matters

  • Divorce financial planning involves assessing and planning for the current and future financial implications of divorce. It encompasses asset division, tax considerations, and post-divorce financial stability.

  • A financial analyst can offer impartial advice on dividing assets, forecast the financial impact of various settlement options, assist in budgeting for post-divorce life, and ensure you’re making informed financial decisions.

  • Consider your current financial assets and liabilities, understand your post-divorce budget needs, assess your future earning potential, and contemplate the tax implications of asset division.

  • Retaining the family home can impact liquidity, future income needs, maintenance costs, and property taxes. It’s crucial to assess whether keeping the home is financially feasible long-term, as well as understanding the true costs of homeownership from a cash flow perspective.

  • Retirement accounts are often considered marital property and may be divided using a Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO). The division depends on the account type and state laws. These are separate legal documents that are drafted in conjunction with your other divorce documents.

  • Marital property is assets acquired during the marriage, while separate property is assets owned before marriage or received as a gift or inheritance. The distinction affects how assets are divided in a divorce. Strada does characterization work to help you understand the difference between the two.

  • Joint debts must be addressed in the divorce settlement. It’s common for debts to be divided in a manner similar to assets, but remember that creditors may still hold both parties responsible for joint debts.

  • A post-divorce financial plan helps you adjust to your new financial reality, ensuring you have a strategy in place to meet your goals and maintain financial stability.

  • Collect records of all assets, debts, tax returns, income sources, insurance policies, estate plans, and any other relevant financial documentation.

  • No. Divorce planning is beneficial for anyone undergoing a divorce, regardless of net worth. We believe everyone can benefit from sound financial planning during divorce.

03 Collaborative Divorce

  • A collaborative law financial neutral is a professional who assists both parties in a collaborative divorce by providing objective financial analysis and advice, without representing either side.

  • Jennifer provides impartial financial expertise, analyzing your combined financial situation, helping identify and value assets, and forecasting the long-term implications of settlement options to facilitate equitable decisions.

  • A financial neutral typically holds certifications such as a Certified Divorce Financial Analyst (CDFA), Certified Financial Planner (CFP), or Certified Public Accountant (CPA) with specialized training in collaborative divorce processes.

  • You’ll need to share comprehensive financial data, including income sources, bank statements, investments, property values, debts, and any other relevant financial documents. This is an integral part of the collaborative process to ensure transparency for all parties.

  • Yes. A financial neutral complements the legal advice from your attorneys by focusing on the financial aspects, ensuring you’re fully informed when making decisions.

  • While financial neutrals can outline potential tax implications of divorce decisions, specific tax advice should be verified with a qualified tax professional or preparer.

  • The financial neutral can present alternative scenarios, facilitate discussions to uncover underlying concerns, and help find creative solutions to break the deadlock.

  • All information shared with a financial neutral is treated with strict confidentiality within the bounds of the collaborative divorce process and applicable laws.

04 Attorney Services

  • A financial analyst provides expert analysis on financial issues, offering detailed reports for asset valuation, income analysis, financial impact forecasts, and more, supporting attorneys in building a stronger case for their clients.

  • Jennifer offers financial analysis in divorce, including asset tracing, valuation, income and expense analysis, settlement scenario forecasting, mediation client support and expert witness testimony.

  • Yes. Financial planners like Jennifer can serve as expert witnesses, providing testimony on financial matters to support case facts.

  • Early involvement allows for thorough financial assessment and strategic planning, giving attorneys clearer financial context and more efficient decision-making.

  • Yes. Attorneys can engage financial professionals on a retainer basis to provide ongoing support for complex cases requiring continuous financial analysis.

  • Comprehensive financial data related to the case, including asset inventories, income statements, tax returns, and any pertinent legal documents.

  • Clear communication, setting mutual expectations early, and regular updates ensure that financial analyses align with legal strategies and case goals.
