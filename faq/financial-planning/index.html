Financial & Retirement Planning

Library 01

← All FAQ categories

  • Creating a roadmap for your financial future, covering aspects like savings, investments, taxes, retirement, and estate planning, tailored to meet your long-term goals.

  • Ensuring you have the necessary resources to support yourself and maintain your desired lifestyle in retirement, without the reliability of a regular paycheck. We help clients manage the transition and stress that often accompanies retirement.

  • Ideally, you should review your financial plan annually or when significant life events occur, such as marriage, the birth of a child, a career change, or inheritance. We meet with clients as frequently as they prefer.

  • The sooner, the better. Starting in your 20s or as soon as you begin earning an income allows more time for your investments to grow through the power of compounding interest. However, it’s never too late to begin planning.

  • A 401(k) is a retirement savings plan offered by employers that often includes a match to your contributions. An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is a retirement saving account you open independently, offering tax advantages.

  • The amount varies based on your lifestyle expectations, location, and when you plan to retire. A common benchmark is 70–80% of pre-retirement income, though we plan for 100%.

  • A Roth IRA allows you to contribute after-tax dollars and withdraw the money tax-free in retirement. A traditional IRA contributes pre-tax dollars, reducing your taxable income now, but you’ll pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement. Strategic use of both can save thousands in taxes.

  • Strategies include downsizing your home, reducing discretionary spending, using senior discounts, and managing healthcare costs carefully.

  • Social Security provides a foundation of income in retirement but shouldn’t be relied upon as the sole source. It’s meant to complement your retirement savings and pension.

  • Inflation reduces the purchasing power of your money over time, meaning you’ll need more money in the future to maintain the same standard of living, making it crucial to plan for inflation.

  • It depends on the interest rates. High-interest debt should generally be paid off first, but it’s also important to contribute enough to your retirement to get any employer match, as that’s essentially free money.

  • Diversification is key. A mix of stocks, bonds, and other investments can help manage risk. It’s also wise to adjust your investment strategy as you get closer to retirement age.

  • Yes, many people choose part-time work or consulting after retirement for both income and personal fulfillment. However, be mindful of how this income may affect your Social Security benefits.

  • A CFP® is a professional with certification in financial planning. This designation helps ensure comprehensive planning aligned with your goals.

  • Look for certifications, a fiduciary commitment, experience relevant to your needs, and a fee structure that fits with your budget and preferences.

  • Starting early gives your investments more time to grow, leveraging the benefit of compounding returns which can significantly increase the size of your retirement fund.

  • Approaches include careful withdrawal planning, considering inflation, maintaining appropriate investment allocation, and potentially delaying Social Security benefits.

  • Estate planning is preparing for the management and distribution of your assets after your death. It’s a critical component of a financial plan, ensuring your assets are distributed according to your wishes.

  • Jennifer offers expertise in comprehensive financial planning and develops tailored retirement strategies with ongoing guidance.

  • Strada Wealth Management offers personalized, client-focused advice without conflicts of interest, ensuring that your plan aligns with your unique goals, values, and circumstances, and adapts as they evolve.
