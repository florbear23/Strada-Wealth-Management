Collaborative Divorce

Library 03

← All FAQ categories

  • A collaborative law financial neutral is a professional who assists both parties in a collaborative divorce by providing objective financial analysis and advice, without representing either side.

  • Jennifer provides impartial financial expertise, analyzing your combined financial situation, helping identify and value assets, and forecasting the long-term implications of settlement options to facilitate equitable decisions.

  • A financial neutral typically holds certifications such as a Certified Divorce Financial Analyst (CDFA), Certified Financial Planner (CFP), or Certified Public Accountant (CPA) with specialized training in collaborative divorce processes.

  • A financial neutral ensures that financial decisions are made based on objective analysis and understanding, helping couples reach fair and sustainable agreements while minimizing emotional conflict.

  • They provide a clear picture of the couple’s finances, suggest equitable ways to split assets considering future needs, and explain the tax implications of various division scenarios.

  • Yes, by analyzing incomes, living expenses, and future needs, a financial neutral can project feasible support scenarios for consideration in the collaborative process.

  • You’ll need to share comprehensive financial data, including income sources, bank statements, investments, property values, debts, and any other relevant financial documents.

  • Yes, by clarifying financial matters upfront and facilitating rational decision-making, a financial neutral can help avoid the delays often associated with financial disagreements in divorce.

  • Employing a financial neutral can ultimately reduce costs by streamlining the financial aspect of the divorce, avoiding prolonged disputes and potential litigation.

  • Yes, a financial neutral complements the legal advice from your attorneys by focusing on the financial aspects, ensuring you’re fully informed when making decisions.

  • While financial neutrals can outline potential tax implications of divorce decisions, specific tax advice should be verified with a qualified tax professional or preparer.

  • The financial neutral collaboratively works with both parties’ attorneys, ensuring financial analyses and proposals align with legal strategies and client interests.

  • Yes, financial neutrals can provide valuable financial advice in various types of divorce processes, though their role is optimized in the collaborative framework.

  • The financial neutral can present alternative scenarios, facilitate discussions to uncover underlying concerns, and help find creative solutions to break the deadlock.

  • The financial neutral analyzes current values, tax impacts, and future growth to recommend equitable ways to divide retirement accounts.

  • They do not decide, but can provide analyses on the affordability and financial impact of keeping the home versus selling it, aiding in informed decision-making.

  • Financial neutrals are adept at analyzing financial disclosures and identifying inconsistencies or gaps, but a forensic accountant may be needed for in-depth investigation of hidden assets.

  • All information shared with a financial neutral is treated with strict confidentiality within the bounds of the collaborative divorce process and applicable laws.

  • After divorce, financial neutrals can help reassess your financial situation and guide the updating of your estate plan to reflect your new status and goals.
